The problem

The published minimum is not the real bar.

  • A program says 500 hours. Its last class averaged 2,900.

    Minimums are legal floors, not admissions targets. Applying at the minimum is how most people spend their money without ever being read.

  • Nobody tells you which hours count.

    Scribing counts at some programs and not others. Same for EMT, CNA, and medical assisting. The distinction is buried in supplemental applications you only see after you have paid.

  • CASPA costs $1,400 for fifteen programs.

    A bad list is the single most expensive mistake in this process — and the most common reason capable applicants reapply.

Questions

Before you buy.

Where does your data actually come from?
Three sources. Program-published materials and CASPA profiles, which we re-check every cycle. Self-reported outcomes from applicants, which we verify against acceptance letters before a record is marked verified. And direct correspondence with admissions offices, which we cite by date.
Is this just the CASPA directory reformatted?
No. CASPA publishes the minimum. We publish what the admitted class actually looked like, which is almost always a different number, and the gap between those two is the entire product.
Will you write or edit my personal statement?
No. We show you twelve admitted essays with annotations explaining what worked, and we will review a draft you wrote. We do not write application material. Programs treat consultant-written essays as fraud, and so do we.
I am a reapplicant. Is this still useful?
It is most useful for reapplicants. The usual reason a second cycle fails is that the list did not change, only the essay did.
What happens to my data?
We store what you enter to run your list and nothing else. We never sell it, never share it with programs, and you can export or delete everything from your account page at any time. Anything that appears in our published statistics is aggregated and never identifies you.
